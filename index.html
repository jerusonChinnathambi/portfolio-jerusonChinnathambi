Flagship project · Consulting engagement · Jan–May 2026

Biryani Heaven.
From POS data to a $22K monthly lift.

An independent restaurant handed me five months of raw point-of-sale exports and one question: how do we grow? I built the full answer — analysis, machine-learning forecasting, and growth campaigns — and May closed at $54,000+, a $22,000+ jump over April.

01 · THE BUSINESS PROBLEMStrong sales volume. Zero visibility.

Problem
The owner couldn't see which days were underperforming, why weekends did better, or how to plan staffing and inventory. Decisions were being made on gut feel.
My job
Turn 4,936 orders of raw POS data into answers: find where revenue was leaking, build a forecast the owner could plan around, and design strategies to grow revenue 15–25% in 6 months.

02 · FINDING THE LEAKSThe data showed exactly where money was being lost.

How
I explored the data with Python, Pandas, Matplotlib and Seaborn — breaking revenue down by day of week, screening anomalies with Z-scores, and quantifying every gap I found.
Found
Weekends earned 41% more than weekdays. Monday was the weakest day, 45% below Saturday. And the biggest leak: closing every Tuesday had cost an estimated $27,719 in five months.

03 · BUILDING THE FORECASTSix models tested. The best one put on the job.

How
I trained six forecasting models with scikit-learn, XGBoost and Prophet, then tested each on a month of data they had never seen — so the owner could trust the numbers, not just hope.
Solved
Gradient Boosting won, predicting daily revenue within about $157 on average. Blended with Prophet, it projected $35,544 for June — turning staffing and inventory from guesswork into a plan.

The forecasting system end to end — raw POS exports flow through feature engineering, model training and real-world testing, into the production forecast the owner plans around.

04 · TURNING INSIGHT INTO REVENUESeven strategies. Every one backed by a number.

How
Each fix targeted a leak the data exposed: Google Ads and social campaigns aimed at the two weakest days, online ordering and catering switched on (the data showed zero delivery revenue), weekend staff trained to upsell, and tip prompts optimized.
Result
May closed at $54,000+ — up $22,000+ from April's $32,422. Average daily revenue grew 43% from January to May, with several days breaking $2,000.

IEEE CLICK2026 · Peer-reviewed publication

FinTech & Financial Inclusion: An AI Framework for Developing Economies

ProblemRoughly 1.4 billion adults worldwide have no access to formal banking — and policymakers lack tools to know which levers actually expand access.
What I didCo-built the DAFFI framework: ensemble machine learning (Random Forest, XGBoost, LSTM) combined with behavioral modeling and NLP sentiment analysis, validated on 2,340 survey respondents across India, Nigeria, Kenya, Brazil, Bangladesh and Indonesia.
What it solvedThe model identified trust, digital literacy and smart regulation as the highest-impact levers — giving central banks a simulator to test policy before spending on it. Published at IEEE CLICK2026 with Dr. S. Chinnathambi.
Read the paper › Marketing analytics · ML forecasting

Red Bull: ML Forecasting for Marketing Investment

ProblemHow much should a global brand spend on marketing over the next five years — and where?
What I didBuilt an ensemble of forecasting models in Python and scikit-learn on 2017–2025 revenue and spend data, with SQL analysis of sales, health-impact and Google Trends datasets.
What it solvedA 2026–2030 investment roadmap: revenue projected to grow from $12.7B to ~$18B, with a recommended $26.5B cumulative marketing plan split 45% sports sponsorships, 30% digital, 15% traditional, 10% brand IP.
Read the paper › Operations & supply chain

Apple Silicon: Fixing a Single Point of Failure

ProblemThe vast majority of Apple's supplier facilities sit in one region — a single point of failure for a trillion-dollar supply chain, exposed by COVID and geopolitics.
What I didAnalyzed Apple's financial reports, supplier-responsibility data and geopolitical risk using Excel, SQL, Power BI and Tableau to map the concentration and its cost.
What it solvedA concrete diversification plan: rebalance Asia to ~40% of supplier footprint, develop Africa and Central America as secondary hubs, and use analytics to cut lead-time volatility.
